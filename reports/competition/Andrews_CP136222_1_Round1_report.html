What Happened This Round

Andrews finished Competition Round 1 profitable at $567 on sales of $129,024 (rank 2 of 8 on sales; profit rank 4 of 8). Cash ended at $4,021 with no emergency loan. Below: what worked, where share lagged, and what to prioritize before Round 2 locks.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash planning held — no emergency loan. You ended with cash $4,021 and no emergency loan. That means your financing and spending roughly matched. Keep that discipline in Round 2 — an emergency loan is Capsim's expensive automatic borrowing when cash would otherwise go negative.
  2. 2. You finished in the black (profit $567). Net profit $567 (rank 4 of 8 on profit). Industry average profit was ($634). Sales were $129,024 vs industry average ~$114,936.
  3. 3. Excess inventory tied up cash on several products. Balance-sheet inventory is $5,732. Largest unit piles: Able (215 units). Unsold units mean cash spent on material and labor is sitting on the shelf instead of in the bank.
  4. 4. Plant investment was $7,400 this round. Net plant improvements used about $7,400 of cash. You issued stock $0 and LTD $5,100. Financing roughly covered the investment — good pattern to keep.
  5. 5. Able earned a strong customer survey score in Traditional. Able CS 56 (awareness 82.8%, accessibility 70.0%). That is a real strength to protect in Round 2.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — serious problems first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Protect liquidity while you invest

You avoided an emergency loan (cash $4,021). Keep that standard. Any capacity/automation spend needs matching financing up front — do not assume operations will refill the account in time.

2 Capture demand you already earned (Acre)

Those products stocked out. Raise the production schedule (and evaluate capacity only if growth looks durable), and make sure sales budgets support accessibility so the extra units can actually sell.

3 Clear excess inventory on slow movers

Cut or carefully right-size production on Able (215 left) until shelves normalize — that frees cash faster than most other moves.

4 Low End cost: raise automation on Acre thoughtfully

Automation next round is 5.0. Low End is a price/cost game — higher automation lowers labor and supports a competitive price with a healthier contribution margin. Finance the upgrade; do not sneak it in without cash.
