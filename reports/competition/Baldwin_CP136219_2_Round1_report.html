Quick glossary used below: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces on you when cash would otherwise go negative — it is expensive and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (driven mainly by promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy your product in the sales channel (driven mainly by sales budget and how many products share that budget). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” circle on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

What to Focus on Next Round

Priorities tailored to Baldwin — concrete problems first, then stretch. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Cash & the emergency loan

Treat the $10,868 emergency loan as a hard lesson, not a verdict. Before Round 2, build a simple cash plan — starting cash, collections, production, SG&A, plant/automation, debt service — and arrange current debt, long-term debt, and/or equity deliberately. Goal: end Round 2 with positive cash and $0 new emergency loan.

2 Clear excess inventory (Bead)

Cut production schedules on overstocked products while you improve positioning, MTBF, or marketing. Cash trapped on the shelf cannot pay for R&D or promotion.

3 Tighten R&D and marketing on soft products

Focus revisions on Bead (Low End), Buddy (Size): move toward each segment’s ideal spot, keep MTBF inside the band, and fund awareness/accessibility enough to compete with leaders.

4 Competitors to watch

Study Chester, Digby, Garrett — not to copy blindly, but to see how they paired financing with investment, and which segments they defended on price vs positioning.

You can do this. Competition Round 1 is graded, and it is also the first real data point in a multi-round story. Teams that read their numbers carefully, fix one or two concrete offer mistakes, and plan cash deliberately often rebound hard in Round 2.
