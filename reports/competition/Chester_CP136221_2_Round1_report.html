What Happened This Round

Sales were $124,868 (industry average about $113,946), net profit (5,876), contribution margin 31.1%, and the stock closed at $19.85 (EPS -2.45). This is graded, not practice — but Round 1 setbacks are highly recoverable when you connect each decision to its outcome.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy your product (mainly sales budget and how many products share it). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Sales were among the industry leaders ($124,868). Customers responded to parts of your lineup. Convert that top-line strength into healthier margins and clean cash — without abandoning what is working.
  2. 2. Emergency loan of $4,508 — Capsim stepped in when cash ran out. Ending cash is $0.00. An emergency loan is automatic expensive short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative. Likely drivers: cash tied up in unsold inventory; operating losses reduced cash generation. Round 2 is absolutely recoverable if you build an explicit cash plan and arrange debt/equity on purpose.
  3. 3. Cent is on the books but did not sell this round. Cent shows capacity next round of 300 but 0 units sold. If this is still in R&D, protect cash until launch. If it was meant to compete now, check price, positioning, and awareness before producing.
  4. 4. SG&A is high relative to sales (22.9%). SG&A (promotion, sales, admin) took 22.9% of sales. Marketing spend is healthy when it buys awareness and accessibility that convert; when sales are soft, the same budgets weigh on profit. Tighten the link between spend and segment goals.
The through-line: cash planning and a few product/offer mismatches created a liquidity gap; Capsim filled it with an expensive emergency loan. Round 2 is recoverable if you fix the broken offers, match production to real demand, and arrange financing deliberately.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — start with cash and broken offers, then growth. These are focus areas, not a full decision set. You’ve got this.

Treat the $4,508 emergency loan as a learning moment, not a verdict.
