What Happened This Round

Chester finished Competition Round 1 with sales of $141,058 (rank 1 of 8) and a net loss of ($2,265), but avoided an emergency loan (cash $15,496). Liquidity held; the work now is margins, mix, and segment fit.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash planning held — no emergency loan. You ended with cash $15,496 and no emergency loan. That means your financing and spending roughly matched. Keep that discipline in Round 2 — an emergency loan is Capsim's expensive automatic borrowing when cash would otherwise go negative.
  2. 2. Profit was negative (($2,265)) — fixable with margin and mix. Sales $141,058 (rank 1 of 8). Contribution margin 29.8% (industry avg 29.1%); SG&A/Sales 20.3%. EBIT was $4,369 before interest of $7,854.
  3. 3. Excess inventory tied up cash on several products. Balance-sheet inventory is $14,542. Largest unit piles: Cake (249 units), Cid (171 units). Unsold units mean cash spent on material and labor is sitting on the shelf instead of in the bank.
  4. 4. Plant investment was $8,400 this round. Net plant improvements used about $8,400 of cash. You issued stock $0 and LTD $18,900. Financing roughly covered the investment — good pattern to keep.
  5. 5. Coat earned a strong customer survey score in Performance. Coat CS 49 (awareness 75.4%, accessibility 57.0%). That is a real strength to protect in Round 2.
  6. 6. Cure earned a strong customer survey score in Size. Cure CS 56 (awareness 75.4%, accessibility 62.0%). That is a real strength to protect in Round 2.
  7. 7. SG&A is high relative to sales. SG&A/Sales 20.3%. High promo/sales spend is fine when it buys awareness and accessibility — audit budgets on products with weak CS or tiny volume.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.

Capability building (automation, possible TQM cost initiatives, targeted R&D) only sticks if cash planning comes first and products sit in the right price/position/MTBF box for their segment. Round 2 is where disciplined teams separate.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — serious problems first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Protect liquidity while you invest

You avoided an emergency loan (cash $15,496). Keep that standard. Any capacity/automation spend needs matching financing up front — do not assume operations will refill the account in time.

2 Capture demand you already earned (Cedar)

Those products stocked out. Raise the production schedule (and evaluate capacity only if growth looks durable), and make sure sales budgets support accessibility so the extra units can actually sell.

3 Clear excess inventory on slow movers

Cut or carefully right-size production on Cake (249 left) until shelves normalize — that frees cash faster than most other moves.

4 Competitors to watch

Ferris led profit ($2,175); in Low End, study Acre (Andrews) — CS 27, sold 2,029; in Traditional, study Able (Andrews) — CS 56, sold 1,404; in Performance, study Foam (Ferris) — CS 41, sold 435. Copy their financing discipline and segment criteria fit — not their logo.
