Quick glossary (first-use Capsim terms): An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy you in the sales channel (mainly sales budget, shared across products). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band. SG&A is selling, general & administrative expense (promotion, sales, admin).

  3. 3. You stocked out where customers wanted more — a good problem, if you respond. Stockouts on Dell mean demand exceeded supply. Raise production (and check capacity), and fund accessibility so you do not leave sales on the table.
  4. 4. Margin and overhead need a closer handshake. Company CM 25.6% and SG&A-to-sales 20.8%. Pair marketing budgets with product fixes — not instead of them.

The through-line: specific product and cash-planning choices created a shortfall Capsim filled with an emergency loan. Round 2 is absolutely recoverable — fix product economics, match production to demand, and arrange financing before cash hits zero.

What this means for next round

Lasting advantage comes from speed (R&D), cost (automation/material/labor), and cash discipline — not from one big promo splash. Pick one or two capability bets that support your best products, and fund them without starving liquidity.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — highest leverage first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set. You’ve got this.

1 Cash & the emergency loan

Treat the $18,367 emergency loan as a loud, useful lesson. Before Round 2, write a simple cash plan — collections, production, SG&A, plant, debt service — and arrange current debt, LTD, and/or equity deliberately. Goal: end Round 2 with positive cash and $0 new emergency loan.

2 Tighten fine-cut fit on your core products

Move each primary product closer to its segment ideal on the highest-weighted criteria (age, positioning, MTBF, or price). Small, correct R&D moves compound.

3 Capture demand you already earned (Dell)

You stocked out — customers wanted more. Raise production schedules, check capacity, and fund accessibility so Round 2 converts that demand into sales and cash.

4 Improve contribution margin where price is the battleground

Company CM is 25.6%. On Low End especially, pair competitive prices with automation and cost control so volume does not become a margin trap.
