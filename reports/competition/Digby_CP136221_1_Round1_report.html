What Happened This Round

Digby finished Competition Round 1 with a hard cash lesson — Capsim advanced an emergency loan of $32,238 and you ended with cash of $0.00. Sales were $71,730 (industry average about $115,191), net profit (11,968), contribution margin 24.6%, and the stock closed at $1.00 (EPS -6.02). This is graded, not practice — but Round 1 setbacks are highly recoverable when you connect each decision to its outcome.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy your product (mainly sales budget and how many products share it). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Emergency loan of $32,238 — Capsim stepped in when cash ran out. Ending cash is $0.00. An emergency loan is automatic expensive short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative. Likely drivers: cash tied up in unsold inventory; operating losses reduced cash generation; early retirement of long-term debt while liquidity was tight. Round 2 is absolutely recoverable if you build an explicit cash plan and arrange debt/equity on purpose.
  2. 2. Daze (Traditional): price $34.00 sits outside the segment band. Traditional customers only treat prices between $19.50–$29.50 as acceptable. At $34.00, Daze sold only 119 units and left 1,234 in inventory, with contribution margin -24.8%. Clear decision→outcome: fix the price/segment match before another large batch.
  3. 3. Dot (Performance): price $34.75 sits outside the segment band. Performance customers only treat prices between $24.50–$34.50 as acceptable. At $34.75, Dot sold only 291 units and left 254 in inventory, with contribution margin 22.5%. Clear decision→outcome: fix the price/segment match before another large batch.
  4. 4. SG&A is high relative to sales (25.9%). SG&A (promotion, sales, admin) took 25.9% of sales. Marketing spend is healthy when it buys awareness and accessibility that convert; when sales are soft, the same budgets weigh on profit. Tighten the link between spend and segment goals.
The through-line: cash planning and a few product/offer mismatches created a liquidity gap; Capsim filled it with an expensive emergency loan. Round 2 is recoverable if you fix the broken offers, match production to real demand, and arrange financing deliberately.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — start with cash and broken offers, then growth. These are focus areas, not a full decision set. You’ve got this.

1 Cash & the emergency loan

Treat the $32,238 emergency loan as a learning moment, not a verdict. Before Round 2, sketch a simple cash plan — starting cash, collections, production, SG&A, plant/automation, debt service — and arrange current debt, long-term debt, and/or equity on purpose. Goal: end Round 2 with positive cash and $0 new emergency loan.

2 Fix Daze's price/segment match

Daze at $34.00 sits outside Traditional's $19.50–$29.50 band (sold 119, inventory 1,234). Decide: price into the band and compete there, or reposition toward a segment where that price can work — and do not produce another large run until the match is coherent.

3 Fix Dot's price/segment match

Dot at $34.75 sits outside Performance's $24.50–$34.50 band (sold 291, inventory 254). Decide: price into the band and compete there, or reposition toward a segment where that price can work — and do not produce another large run until the match is coherent.

4 Work down excess inventory

Daze is carrying heavy ending inventory. Cut schedules until shelves clear — cash trapped in inventory cannot pay for marketing, plant, or debt service.

5 Chase fine-cut fit on your core products

For each active product, move toward the segment ideal on the criteria that matter most (age & price in Traditional/Low End; positioning & age in High End/Size; MTBF & positioning in Performance). Small, consistent R&D revisions beat one dramatic gamble.
