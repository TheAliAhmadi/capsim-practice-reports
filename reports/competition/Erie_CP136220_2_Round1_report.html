Quick glossary (first-use Capsim terms): An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy you in the sales channel (mainly sales budget, shared across products). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” on the perceptual map for a segment; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band. SG&A is selling, general & administrative expense (promotion, sales, admin).

  1. 1. Cash buffer — a quiet strength. Ending cash of $13,194 and no emergency loan give you room to invest. Keep matching plant and inventory spending to financing.
  2. 2. You stocked out where customers wanted more — a good problem, if you respond. Stockouts on Ebb, Edge mean demand exceeded supply. Raise production (and check capacity), and fund accessibility so you do not leave sales on the table.
  3. 3. You invested in the plant — finance it on purpose. Net plant improvements used about $9,840 (stock issued $0, LTD issued $4,100). Keep the financing side as intentional as the spending side.

Bright spot: a profitable Round 1. Protect that edge with disciplined cash and inventory.

1 Protect liquidity while you invest

You avoided an emergency loan (ending cash $13,194). Keep that edge: any capacity/automation spend in Round 2 should arrive with matching financing.

2 Tighten fine-cut fit on your core products

3 Capture demand you already earned (Ebb, Edge)

Company CM is 29.7%. On Low End especially, pair competitive prices with automation and cost control so volume does not become a margin trap.
