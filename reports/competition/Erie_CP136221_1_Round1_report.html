A modest loss in Round 1 is common and fixable. Below we translate the numbers into clear coaching moves — warmly, and without shame — so your team knows exactly what to try next.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is for customers to find and buy your product (mainly sales budget and how many products share it). A fine cut is the “sweet spot” circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Sales were among the industry leaders ($125,198). Customers responded to parts of your lineup. Convert that top-line strength into healthier margins and clean cash — without abandoning what is working.
  2. 2. Easy is on the books but did not sell this round. Easy shows capacity next round of 600 but 0 units sold. If this is still in R&D, protect cash until launch. If it was meant to compete now, check price, positioning, and awareness before producing.
  3. 3. Eat (Traditional): customers wanted more than you supplied. Eat stocked out (sold ~1,329; potential closer to ~1,440). That is a supply opportunity — raise the production schedule (and evaluate capacity if the pattern continues) so you capture sales you already earned.

The through-line: Round 1 results came from specific decisions — pricing, positioning, production, marketing, and financing — not from luck. Tighten those levers and the scoreboard can move quickly.
