What Happened This Round

Erie finished Competition Round 1 profitable at $1,627 on sales of $114,322 (rank 4 of 8 on sales; profit rank 2 of 8). Cash ended at $11,811 with no emergency loan. Below: what worked, where share lagged, and what to prioritize before Round 2 locks.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash planning held — no emergency loan. You ended with cash $11,811 and no emergency loan. That means your financing and spending roughly matched. Keep that discipline in Round 2 — an emergency loan is Capsim's expensive automatic borrowing when cash would otherwise go negative.
  2. 2. You finished in the black (profit $1,627). Net profit $1,627 (rank 2 of 8 on profit). Industry average profit was ($634). Sales were $114,322 vs industry average ~$114,936.
  3. 3. You stocked out where customers wanted more. Stockouts on Ebb mean demand exceeded supply — a production/capacity scheduling opportunity, not a 'nobody likes the product' story. Lean inventory overall ($7,653) is good; just don't leave paid demand on the table.
  4. 4. Plant investment was $11,200 this round. Net plant improvements used about $11,200 of cash. You issued stock $0 and LTD $6,100. Financing roughly covered the investment — good pattern to keep.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.

What this means for next round

Capability building (automation, possible TQM cost initiatives, targeted R&D) only sticks if cash planning comes first and products sit in the right price/position/MTBF box for their segment. Round 2 is where disciplined teams separate.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — serious problems first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Protect liquidity while you invest

You avoided an emergency loan (cash $11,811). Keep that standard. Any capacity/automation spend needs matching financing up front — do not assume operations will refill the account in time.

2 Capture demand you already earned (Ebb)

Those products stocked out. Raise the production schedule (and evaluate capacity only if growth looks durable), and make sure sales budgets support accessibility so the extra units can actually sell.

3 Tighten Echo in High End

Focus on the top buying criteria for High End (Positioning 43% | Age 29% | MTBF 19% | Price 9%). One or two decisive fixes beat changing every dial.

4 Competitors to watch

Chester led industry sales ($141,058); Ferris led profit ($2,175); in Low End, study Acre (Andrews) — CS 27, sold 2,029; in Traditional, study Able (Andrews) — CS 56, sold 1,404. Copy their financing discipline and segment criteria fit — not their logo.
