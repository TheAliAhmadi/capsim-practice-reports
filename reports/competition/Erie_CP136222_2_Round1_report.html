What Happened This Round

Erie finished Competition Round 1 with an emergency loan of $15,855 and a net loss of ($8,677). Sales were $86,144 (rank 7 of 7). That is a tough opening graded round — and it is also highly fixable. The patterns below are concrete decision → outcome links, not a verdict on your team.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash ran short — Capsim issued an emergency loan. Ending cash was $0 and Capsim advanced an emergency loan of $15,855. Likely drivers: a large inventory balance ($22,569 on the balance sheet; about 1,139 unsold units across products) locked cash on the shelf; heavy plant spending (~$31,420) raised the cash need sharply; an operating loss of ($8,677) reduced cash generated from the business. Round 2 is recoverable: build an explicit cash plan and arrange current debt, LTD, and/or equity before cash hits zero.
  2. 2. Profit was negative (($8,677)) — fixable with margin and mix. Sales $86,144 (rank 7 of 7). Contribution margin 26.2% (industry avg 27.9%); SG&A/Sales 18.3%. EBIT was ($3,315) before interest of $10,035.
  3. 3. Excess inventory tied up cash on several products. Balance-sheet inventory is $22,569. Largest unit piles: Eat (716 units), Echo (169 units), Egg (178 units). Unsold units mean cash spent on material and labor is sitting on the shelf instead of in the bank.
  4. 4. Plant investment was $31,420 this round. Net plant improvements used about $31,420 of cash. You issued stock $13,600 and LTD $18,900. Financing roughly covered the investment — good pattern to keep.
  5. 5. SG&A is high relative to sales. SG&A/Sales 18.3%. High promo/sales spend is fine when it buys awareness and accessibility — audit budgets on products with weak CS or tiny volume.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. Stabilize cash first, then defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — serious problems first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Cash plan — retire the emergency-loan habit

You carried an emergency loan of $15,855. Before Round 2, sketch starting cash, collections, production spend, SG&A, plant/automation, and debt service — then issue current debt, LTD, and/or equity deliberately. Goal: end Round 2 with positive cash and $0 new emergency loan.

2 Capture demand you already earned (Ebb)

Those products stocked out. Raise the production schedule (and evaluate capacity only if growth looks durable), and make sure sales budgets support accessibility so the extra units can actually sell.

3 Clear excess inventory on slow movers

Cut or carefully right-size production on Eat (716 left) until shelves normalize — that frees cash faster than most other moves.

4 Tighten Eat in Traditional

Focus on the top buying criteria for Traditional (Age 47% | Price 23% | Positioning 21% | Reliability 9%). One or two decisive fixes beat changing every dial.

Baldwin led industry sales ($133,451); Ferris led profit ($2,555); in Low End, study Acre (Andrews) — CS 29, sold 2,099; in Traditional, study Able (Andrews) — CS 45, sold 1,410. Copy their financing discipline and segment criteria fit — not their logo.
