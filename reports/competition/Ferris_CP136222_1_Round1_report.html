What Happened This Round

Ferris remained profitable ($2,175) but needed an emergency loan of $1,335 because cash planning and spending did not fully line up. Sales $120,178 (rank 3 of 8). Treat the loan as a financing lesson, not a strategy death sentence.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash ran short — Capsim issued an emergency loan. Ending cash was $0 and Capsim advanced an emergency loan of $1,335. Likely drivers: net cash from operations was negative (($4,766)). Round 2 is recoverable: build an explicit cash plan and arrange current debt, LTD, and/or equity before cash hits zero.
  2. 2. You finished in the black (profit $2,175). Net profit $2,175 (rank 1 of 8 on profit). Industry average profit was ($634). Sales were $120,178 vs industry average ~$114,936.
  3. 3. Excess inventory tied up cash on several products. Balance-sheet inventory is $12,529. Largest unit piles: Feat (270 units), Fume (168 units). Unsold units mean cash spent on material and labor is sitting on the shelf instead of in the bank.
  4. 4. Fume earned a strong customer survey score in Size. Fume CS 54 (awareness 66.4%, accessibility 48.0%). That is a real strength to protect in Round 2.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. Stabilize cash first, then defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.

What to Focus on Next Round

Five priorities — serious problems first. These are focus areas, not a full decision set.

1 Cash plan — retire the emergency-loan habit

You carried an emergency loan of $1,335. Before Round 2, sketch starting cash, collections, production spend, SG&A, plant/automation, and debt service — then issue current debt, LTD, and/or equity deliberately. Goal: end Round 2 with positive cash and $0 new emergency loan.

2 Clear excess inventory on slow movers

Cut or carefully right-size production on Feat (270 left) until shelves normalize — that frees cash faster than most other moves.

3 Tighten Fume in Size

Focus on the top buying criteria for Size (Positioning 43% | Age 29% | MTBF 19% | Price 9%). One or two decisive fixes beat changing every dial.

4 Low End cost: raise automation on Feat thoughtfully

Automation next round is 5.0. Low End is a price/cost game — higher automation lowers labor and supports a competitive price with a healthier contribution margin. Finance the upgrade; do not sneak it in without cash.
