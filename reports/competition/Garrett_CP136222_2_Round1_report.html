What Happened This Round

Garrett remained profitable ($48) but needed an emergency loan of $3,993 because cash planning and spending did not fully line up. Sales $119,389 (rank 3 of 7). Treat the loan as a financing lesson, not a strategy death sentence.

Quick glossary: An emergency loan is automatic short-term borrowing Capsim forces when cash would otherwise go negative — expensive, and a signal that financing and cash planning did not match spending. Contribution margin (CM) is the percent of each sales dollar left after variable production costs (material, labor, inventory carrying) — before SG&A, depreciation, and interest. Awareness is the share of customers who know your product exists (mainly promotion budget). Accessibility is how easy it is to find and buy you in the channel (mainly sales budget / how many products share it). A fine cut is the "sweet spot" circle on the perceptual map; customers strongly prefer products inside it. MTBF (Mean Time Between Failure) is reliability in hours; each segment has a required band.

  1. 1. Cash ran short — Capsim issued an emergency loan. Ending cash was $0 and Capsim advanced an emergency loan of $3,993. Likely drivers: plant investment of about $15,280 was only partly covered by new financing (stock issued $0, LTD issued $5,000). Round 2 is recoverable: build an explicit cash plan and arrange current debt, LTD, and/or equity before cash hits zero.
  2. 2. You finished in the black (profit $48). Net profit $48 (rank 5 of 7 on profit). Industry average profit was ($1,015). Sales were $119,389 vs industry average ~$113,195.
  3. 3. Excess inventory tied up cash on several products. Balance-sheet inventory is $13,780. Largest unit piles: Gerbil (597 units). Unsold units mean cash spent on material and labor is sitting on the shelf instead of in the bank.
  4. 4. Plant investment was $15,280 this round. Net plant improvements used about $15,280 of cash. You issued stock $0 and LTD $5,000. When plant spend exceeds new financing, cash stress (or an emergency loan) often follows.
The through-line: Round 1 is graded, but it is still early. Stabilize cash first, then defend your strongest segments, fix the clearest product gaps, and match production to real demand. You do not need a miracle — you need a short list of disciplined Round 2 decisions.
